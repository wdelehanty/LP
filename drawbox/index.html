The problem

Every boat parks the cooler under a seat. Then the seat is in the way.

On a center console it's wedged under the leaning post, so people spend $300 on a slide tray and still have to drag it out and pop the lid. On a bowrider it's in the box under the passenger console seat, so you pull the cushion or tilt the whole cooler to get a drink. On a pontoon the factory "cooler" is an uninsulated liner under a cushion someone is sitting on.

  • The drawer is the cooler. Nothing to drag out, no lid to clear, nobody gets up
  • Slides all the way out, so you see everything instead of digging
  • Three sizes, built to the three cavities boats actually have

How it installs

Bolt it in, or cut one rectangle.

Post bolts to the leaning post legs and the deck, the same way a slide tray does. Console and Base go into a rectangular cutout, and the face frame covers the edge so the cut doesn't have to be pretty. Ice melt stays in a sealed tub with a plug in the floor, so you dump it over the side instead of plumbing a drain or letting it find the bilge.

  • Drill, jigsaw for the cutout versions, and an afternoon
  • Tub lifts out for cleaning or to swap in a dry box
  • Check what's behind the panel first. Fuel tanks and wiring hide under decks and in consoles

Questions

The things people ask first.

Won't the cold fall out every time I open it?

Cold air sinks, so a drawer keeps it in the tub the same way a chest cooler does. A side-opening door is the one that dumps it. We're testing ice loss per opening against a regular top-loader and will publish the numbers before we take a dollar.

Why not a tilt-out, like a kitchen bin?

We prototyped it. Tipping a tub with an inch of meltwater in it pours the water toward the lip, and the cheap hinges that make tip-outs cheap are rated for sponges, not 40 pounds of ice. Slide-out keeps the tub level.

Will it fit my boat?

Post needs a leaning post with 24 inches between the legs and 17 inches from deck to seat bottom. Console needs a hollow console seat box around 23 by 14 by 17 inches inside. Base needs a 27 inch or wider pontoon seat base. Send the measurements below and we'll tell you.

Can I install it myself?

Yes. Post bolts to the post legs and deck the same way a slide tray does. Console and Base need a rectangular cutout with a jigsaw. Check what's behind the panel before you cut; some boats have the fuel tank a few inches under the deck and consoles are full of wiring.

My cooler is under the passenger console. Is that the Console version?

If it's a hollow box under the seat you reach from the bow walkway, yes. On bigger dual consoles the port console is usually the head, with a toilet in it, and that's not a cooler bay. Measure the opening and send it; we'll tell you which one fits or if neither does.

Why is the pontoon one so shallow?

Standard pontoon seat bases are 11 inches tall. After the floor rails and the drawer body, there's about 7 inches inside. That holds a full layer of cans standing up, but not cans and a bag of ice. So Base uses a frozen gel floor and pre-chilled cans instead, which is how a bar fridge drawer works. If you want a real ice chest on a pontoon, it needs a taller lounge base or an in-floor spot, and we'd rather tell you that than sell you something that doesn't work.

Is this a fridge?

No. It's an ice cooler. No wiring, no battery drain, nothing to break. If you want a powered drawer, Dometic and Isotherm make good ones at two to eight times the price.
