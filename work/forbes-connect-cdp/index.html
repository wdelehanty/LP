Forbes Connect on the CDP

An owned-media conversion layer on an 18M+ profile CDP: readers become identified profiles, profiles become segments, and segments route into lifecycle and sales systems.

Problem

A media site knows its audience mostly in aggregate. Pageviews tell you what got read, and almost nothing about who is in market. Moving the business from pageview-based to identity-based monetization needed a layer that could turn anonymous readers into known profiles and put those profiles to work.

What I built

  • Launched Forbes Connect as the conversion layer on a BlueConic CDP holding 18M+ profiles.
  • Behavioral segmentation, so audiences are defined by what people actually read and do on site.
  • On-site dialogues: targeted prompts that convert anonymous readers into identified profiles at the moment their behavior signals interest.
  • Progressive profiling, asking for a little at a time instead of a twelve-field form nobody fills out.
  • Lifecycle routing into Pardot and Salesforce, so an identified profile lands in nurture or in front of a seller without manual handling.
  • The audience intelligence framework around it: predictive LTV, third-party enrichment, and cross-product lifecycle routing.

Stack

blueconic / pardot / salesforce / ga4 / bigquery

Outcome

18M+ profiles under behavioral segmentation, feeding the lifecycle portfolio and CRM routing that the demand engine runs on. The number is the scale of the CDP, and I want to be precise about it: profiles are not leads. The value is that every downstream program starts from identity instead of a cold list.

What a dialogue isAn on-site prompt targeted by behavior. A reader three stories deep into one topic sees a different ask than a first-time visitor. That targeting is why they convert.
Why progressive profilingOne field per interaction. The profile fills in over visits, and completion holds up because nobody ever faces the long form.
