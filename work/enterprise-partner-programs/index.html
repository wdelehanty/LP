Enterprise partner programs

Lead generation programs architected for T-Mobile, ServiceNow, and Indeed: integrated content, research, retargeting, and delivery that lands in the partner's CRM ready to work.

Problem

An enterprise partner buying lead generation does not want a spreadsheet of names. They want qualified people, reached through several channels in a coherent sequence, delivered in a shape their CRM and sellers can act on the same week. Each of these programs had to be architected, sold through internally, and operated.

What I built

  • For T-Mobile: a two-tier product structure, so the program could serve different buyer depths at different price points.
  • For ServiceNow: an integrated partnership spanning content, research, and retargeting, with a year-end insight report as the flagship deliverable that gave the whole program a spine.
  • For Indeed: enterprise lead generation on the same architecture.
  • Across all three: multi-channel orchestration and CRM-ready delivery, because a lead that needs manual cleanup is a lead that goes cold.

Stack

salesforce / gam / linkedin ads / bombora

Outcome

Three named enterprise partners ran programs built on this architecture. The revenue attached to them belongs to Forbes and stays off this site; what I can show is the structure, which is the part that transfers to any company selling to enterprise buyers.

The flagship artifactThe ServiceNow year-end insight report is the pattern I reuse everywhere: give a program one flagship deliverable and every channel underneath it gets easier to justify.
