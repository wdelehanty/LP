Operating principles

Seven of them, each with the receipts elsewhere on this site.

Build the system the campaign leaves behind.

Campaigns end and take their learnings with them unless there is a system to catch what they taught. I judge a quarter by what got built into the machine, and the machine is what I hand to whoever comes after me.

Show the source.

Every number I report carries how it was measured, including the ones on this site. An impressive number with a vague method is a liability sitting in a QBR waiting to go off.

The playbook is what scales, so write it down.

Two people run ten or more concurrent lifecycle programs because the standards, checklists, and prompt libraries exist on paper instead of in anyone's head. Headcount asks come after the playbook is real, and they get smaller.

Rails before agents.

The prompt libraries, QA checklists, and governance existed before any AI touched production work. AI multiplies whatever process it lands in, including a bad one.

Learn enough to ship it yourself.

When design or engineering bandwidth disappeared, I taught myself HTML and CSS and shipped the pages. The same reflex later built a voice agent, an automation stack, and this site.

The sales handoff is a product.

Speed to lead, routing rules, and the one-click outreach library are features of that product, and sellers are its users. Adoption follows when the thing lives where they already work.

Take it apart before you trust it.

Cars, martech stacks, attribution models, chicken coops. Understanding what is inside is the difference between operating a thing and depending on it.
